### How this was checked

**Static scan** of the 16 in-scope page files plus `header.html`, covering:
- em dashes
- arrows and generic CTAs
- buzzwords and statistics
- focus styles and reduced-motion
- labels, `alt` text, `rel` on new-tab links
- fonts and colours

**Live pass** on the real site with Playwright, desktop 1440 and iPhone 15 Plus:
- **Screenshots** of every in-scope page, scrolled slowly first so reveal animations finish.
- **Contrast** computed on the rendered backgrounds, with key pairs re-checked in the antislop contrast checker.
- **Keyboard tab-walk** of every page.
- **Links:** all 137 unique links fetched; failures re-checked in a real browser.
- **Buttons and modals:** every `#` button clicked, and every modal opened and closed with Escape.
- **Forms:** submitted empty only. All non-GET requests were blocked at the network layer, so **no form data reached the Google Sheet, ActiveCampaign or any other endpoint.**
- **Reduced motion:** `prefers-reduced-motion: reduce` emulated.

Anything I only read in code, without watching it happen live, is marked *(code inspection)*.

### Design Read
Reconstructed; there is no DESIGN.md.
> B2B/B2G leadership-development site for K-12 and higher-ed executives, in a classic-authority editorial style (navy / gold / cream, a display serif plus a clean sans, gold italic accent phrases). **Dials: ENERGY 2 / RHYTHM 2 / MOTION 2.**

**17. Em dashes on every page. R-02.**
- **The problem:** 218 sentences use an em dash, 260 dash characters in all, counting ones in code comments.
- **By page:**

  | Page | Sentences |

- **The proposal:** Appendix A lists every sentence with a proposed rewrite. The rewrites change punctuation only: comma, colon, period, parentheses or a middle dot. Wording is unchanged.
- **Testimonials:** 13 rows are inside quotes from real people. They're marked **(quote)**. Changing punctuation inside a quote is normal editing, but the client may want the speaker to OK it.
- *Owner:* client approves the wording, then I apply it. *Effort:* M.

**18. P2P: white text on the gold call-to-action band. R-25.**
- **Where:** the closing "Start Collaborating on Purpose" band, its "Register for Oct 7 →" and "Coffee with Dr. Joe" buttons, the gold sponsor ticker at the top, and the "Next Session" badge.
- **Contrast:** 2.29:1, which fails even the large-text threshold.
- **Cause:** the CSS was written with navy text (`.cta-h`, `.cta-p`, `.btn-gold`: `color:var(--navy)`, lines 221, 509–520). But the page's own reset at `p2p-duda-inject.html:56–67` forces every element to inherit white with `!important`, which overrides the navy.
- **Fix:** scope the reset so the gold sections keep their intended navy. Navy on gold measures **7.98:1**.
- **Effect:** this restores the original design rather than changing it. *Owner:* me. *Effort:* S.

**19. Contrast failures across the site. R-25.**
113 distinct text styles fail WCAG AA on their real background (desktop, measured live). They fall into four clusters.

- **Faint grey labels on navy (1.9–3.9:1)**
  - Where:
    - Home "See how the assessment works…" (1.89:1), "The Brilliant Team Paradox" (1.99:1) and source lines (2.48:1)
    - Burnout Force and TQ Advantage / Team Institute panel labels (2.68–3.74:1)

- **Gold used as text on white or cream (1.6–2.3:1)**
  - Where:
    - Bio card eyebrows and pull-quote attribution

## D. Small UX touches people will notice

Each of these is tied to something observed on this site, not a generic checklist.

**43. Pressed state on buttons.**
- **What's missing:** no page except TQ Assessment has an `:active` style, so on a phone a tap gives no feedback until the next page loads.
- **Fix:** a 1px press-down (`transform: translateY(1px)`) plus a slightly darker fill on all buttons.

**44. Autofill on every form.**
- **What's missing:** no field anywhere has `autocomplete`, so iPhone autofill doesn't offer name or email.
- **Where:** the Home, Burnout Force, P2P and Research forms.
- **Fix:** add `autocomplete="given-name" / "family-name" / "email" / "organization" / "tel"`, and `inputmode="email"` / `"tel"`. Visitors fill the form in two taps.

**45. Anchor jumps that clear the header.**
- **What's missing:** no page sets `scroll-margin-top`.
- **Where it shows:** jump targets such as Home `#solutions` land with their heading under the 60px mobile header.
- **Fix:** `scroll-margin-top: 80px` on section targets.

**46. A cue on sideways-scrolling strips.** The Research stat bar and filter pills, and the Bookstore tabs, are cut off at the right edge with nothing telling people to swipe. A 24px fade on the right edge would make it obvious.

**47. Tap wording on Home's paths list.** "Hover down to see how each path works" (`home-duda-inject.html:1097`) is written for a mouse. Suggest: "Tap or hover a path to see how it works".

**48. Removed** (applied only to The Group's form).

**49. Popup: keep focus inside, then return it.**
- **What happens now:** the Burnout Force Check popup on Home lets Tab leave the dialog, and focus isn't returned to where you were when it closes (audit-001 #8, still open).
- **Also:** its close button is 30×30px (`:1680`); make it 44×44px.

**50. Bigger tap areas on inline links.** Inline "Take the assessment →" links (14px tall) and email / "Read the research" links (17–22px tall) are hard to hit with a thumb. Padding brings them to 44px without changing how they look.

**51. Say when a link opens a new tab.** Amazon, Lulu, Zoom, Calendly and YouTube links open a new tab with no warning. Add a small ↗ plus visually hidden "(opens in a new tab)" text, only on external links.

**52. Blog cards as one link.**
- **What happens now:** each Blog card has several separate links (image, title, "Continue Reading"). Keyboard users press Tab three times per post, and a screen reader hears each title three times.
- **Fix:** make the whole card one link.

**53. Link "Source:" lines to their source.** Home, The Proof, Burnout Force and Speaker print "Source: …" as plain text. Linking each to its report, or to the Research page, turns a claim into evidence at no design cost.
